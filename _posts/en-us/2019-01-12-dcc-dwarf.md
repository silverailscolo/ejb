The Digitrax SMBK can be dressed as an N-scale [main and distant signal](<(../rio_grande_signal/)>). The manual suggests to make it into a main signal by chopping off the top, but it would be a shame not to use the rest on the track.
In this post I will explain how I converted a Digitrax Signal Mast Base Kit mast into a dwarf signal _plus_ a single head signal.

## Supplies

- A [Digitrax SMBK mast](https://www.digitrax.com/tsd/product/SMBK/)
- Thin, flexible wires, 3 colors, each 50mm/2" long
- Brass round tube Ø 2.5 - 3 mm
- Small 10W soldering iron and resin core solder

## Cut off the SMBK top

- Cut the upper head from the mast base using a hand saw or motor tool.

## Wiring

- Clamp the separate signal head PCB in a mini-vise
- Solder three wires of different colors on the three soldering surfaces, as shown in the previous photo
- The mast will function as Ground return. Solder a wire to the bottom end after the next step

## The dwarf mast

- File away one side of the brass tube over a length of 4 mm
- Cut the profile to approx. 40 mm (depending on the thickness of your track)
- Feed the wires through the tube and stick the signal head with 3-component glue

## Connection to the main mast

- Solder 4 wires to the solder tabs of the original mast, so that we can light the dwarf signal.
- Use a 4-pin cable plug to connect both masts under the layout

## The signal head cover

- Typical for the Rio Grande signals is the long hood, the so-called Darth Vader
- Make it from styrene
- Paint the mast, the faceplate and the hood matt black

## More

- Connect the signal to the flat cable from the Digitrax SE8C DCC Signal Decoder.
- Control the SMBK masts with [JMRI](jmri.org) according to a prototype of your choice.
- The complete SMBK manual can be found [here](https://www.digitrax.com/tsd/product/SMBK/)
